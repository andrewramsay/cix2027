## The 1st Conference on Computational Interaction 2027 (CompInt’27)

### Cambridge, UK, June 2027

The ACM Conference on Computational Interaction (CompInt) invites submissions for the papers track. CompInt will be launched in 2027 and aims to become the premier forum for research on computational models of human-computer interaction. 

Building on the success of a decade of the summer school on Computational Interaction and recent symposium, CompInt brings together people who care about computationally implementable methods and models that represent interaction, drive adaptation, support the engineering and design of interactive systems, and provide fundamental insights into interaction. The program is built to engage deeply on each paper contribution and provide opportunities to discuss with world-class students and scholars in the area. 

The inaugural conference will be held at the University of Cambridge in June 2027, in conjunction with the 11th Summer School on Computational Interaction. The size of the conference audience is capped at 120. 

## Call for contributions

We invite contributions that advance understanding of computational approaches that enhance or optimize interaction with humans. A key question is how to represent information about humans, technology, and the environment algorithmically.  Computational interaction often involves elements from machine learning, signal processing, information theory, optimisation, inference, control theory and formal modelling. However, unlike core AI and machine learning conferences, algorithms are studied in the service of human-computer interaction or human-human interaction. CompInt specifically seeks general contributions that reach beyond a single system or study, specifically by illuminating generally applicable principles and methods. 

Suitable contributions should centre formal (i.e. mathematical and computational) models which represent the process of interaction.

Contribution types include but are not limited to:
 * Computational models for representing and providing insight and understanding of interaction
 * Computational methods and models for planning, inferring, and controlling interaction
 * Computational methods that enhance human capabilities via interaction
 * Computational methods for offline analysis and design of interaction
 * Computational methods for online adaptation, assistance, and personalization
 * Computational models that are calibrated and compared against human interactive behavior
 * Empirical studies that directly inform the design of better algorithms for computational interaction
 * Visualisations and transformations of data for understanding interactive systems with a computational basis
 * Metrics, measures, and benchmarks related to the above
 * Workflows for analysis, design, engineering, and deployment of computational methods  
 * Software, architectures, and toolkits that support the application of methods for computational interaction

**Submissions which are out-of-scope will be desk rejected**. This includes general HCI topics without a clear, central computational model, including submissions that are primarily on the integration or study of an AI system in human-computer interaction, or which focus primarily on empirical user studies outside of a computational model or framework.

We invite theoretical contributions as well as traditional systems-focused or empirical-results based contributions. The conference emphasises concise, conceptually coherent papers focusing on a single contribution.
 
## Submissions

We invite 4 (short) or 8 (long) page papers in the 2-column format (excluding references) of the ACM, without appendices. We strongly encourage open science practices and the submission of supplementary code and data; consideration will be given to the availability of such materials in reviewing. Papers will be reviewed by an expert programme committee and external reviewers.

All contributions must be original. They cannot have been published in another peer-reviewed venue or be under review simultaneously. In addition, authors must avoid writing central parts of their manuscripts using LLMs. 

All required materials must be submitted electronically by the abstract and paper deadline.
